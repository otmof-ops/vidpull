# Trademark policy

**vidpull** and the vidpull logo (when there is one) are trademarks of Jay
Taylor. The code is BSD-3-Clause, and clause 3 of that license says so itself:
neither the name of the copyright holder nor the names of contributors may be
used to endorse or promote products derived from this software without
permission. This page says what that means in practice, so nobody has to guess.

The short version: use the name to say what your thing is made of or works
with; do not use it to say what your thing *is*.

## Free to do, no permission needed

- Say that a tool **works with vidpull**, **wraps vidpull**, **extends
  vidpull** or **is based on vidpull**, when that is accurate.
- Write presets, scripts, packaging, desktop or editor integrations and
  teaching material for it. The command's flags, the settings keys and the
  file names are not claimed as trademarks or as protected expression.
- Use the name in articles, talks, tutorials, comparisons, reviews and
  criticism, including unflattering ones.
- Distribute unmodified official releases under the vidpull name, with
  `LICENSE` intact, as the license requires.
- Name a package, plugin or repository in a way that describes its
  relationship truthfully, such as `vidpull-presets-acme`, `vidpull-aur` or
  `awesome-vidpull`, provided it is clear the project is not the official one.

## Ask first

- Naming a **fork, product, service, company or domain** "vidpull" or a
  confusingly similar name (vidpul, vid-pull, vidpuller, vidpull.app). Forks
  are welcome under BSD-3-Clause; they need their own name once they differ
  from the official releases.
- Using the **logo** anywhere other than to link to the official project.
- Calling a build, distribution or service **official**, **certified**, or
  otherwise implying endorsement.
- Merchandise carrying the name or logo.

Open an issue at https://github.com/otmof-ops/vidpull/issues with what you
have in mind. The usual answer to a truthful, non-confusing use is yes.

## House style, which keeps the mark a mark

- vidpull is one word, all lower case, written the way the command is, even
  at the start of a sentence. Use it as the name of this project ("the vidpull
  app", "a vidpull preset"), not as a verb or a generic noun for downloaders.
- The command and the package are `vidpull`; the settings file is
  `config.toml`. Neither is claimed as a mark.
- The first prominent use in a document may carry ™ (or ® once a
  registration exists; none is claimed as registered today). After that,
  plain text.

## Why this exists

BSD-3-Clause lets anyone copy, change and sell the code. It does not let
anyone sell it as vidpull, or pass their own work off as this project, and
its copyright-notice condition keeps the author's credit on every copy. This
policy is the part of that arrangement the license leaves to the trademark
owner. It is written to be easy to comply with, and it is not a license to
the software itself: that is `LICENSE`.
